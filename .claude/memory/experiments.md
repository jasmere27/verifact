## Results
- **E2 run 1 (2026-09-30), EXPERIMENT RESULT: FAIL.** 18 CA employment pages from 18 law-firm sites (7 more blocked fetching); 124 statements: 69 consistent, 26 requires review, 19 unable to verify, 10 potentially unsupported, 0 potentially outdated. 0 fabricated citations (enforced in code). Self-review of the 10 "unsupported" flags against cited excerpts (not attorney-verified): 3 clearly real (final pay "within 72 hours of notice"; sick leave paid at termination ×2), 1 imprecise wording worth review (liquidated damages "twice"), 6 false (source silent ≠ conflict ×5; an old bill version treated as law ×1) → ~35–40% precision vs 70% bar. Fixes: flags now require the source's verbatim conflicting words (checked in code), silence → UNABLE_TO_VERIFY, bill texts excluded as sources.
- **E2 run 2 (2026-09-30), EXPERIMENT RESULT: PASS (preliminary).** 20 pages from 20 law-firm sites (4 blocked); 148 statements: 81 consistent, 29 requires review, 32 unable to verify, 3 potentially outdated, 3 potentially unsupported. 0 fabricated citations. Self-review of the 6 flags: 5 real (hr.law $16.00 minimum wage ×2 vs $16.90 from 2026-01-01; justicelawcorp.com software exemption $56.97 vs $58.85 for 2026; bryanschwartzlaw.com "liquidated damages … twice the unpaid wages" vs statute "equal to"; ferrarovega.com sick leave paid at termination vs DIR "no requirement"), 1 arguable (SB 294 "in effect since January 1" vs obligations from 2026-02-01) → **83% precision** (≥70% bar). Trade-off: recall dropped (e.g. run 1's real "72 hours of notice" issue now lands in REQUIRES_REVIEW). Caveats: only 6 flags; reviewed by the model against cited excerpts, not by an attorney; one state, one practice area. Reports: `experiments/e2-run2/` (local only, gitignored: they name real firms).
